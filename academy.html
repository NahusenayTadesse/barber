An honest fit

Who does well here.

Not everyone should train as a barber, and not everyone who should would enjoy training here. The people who get the most out of this place tend to have a few things in common.

  • They bought clippers long before they bought a course. Cutting mates' hair in a kitchen for free counts for far more than most people give it credit for.
  • They'd rather be corrected on the spot than told they're doing fine. Small groups exist so someone can watch your hands and say something. That only helps people who want to hear it.
  • They care about being employable in twelve weeks, not about a certificate to frame. The final weeks are about speed, because speed is what a shop actually hires for.
  • They want textured and Afro hair taught as core craft. Braiding, relaxing, colour and pattern work sit in the syllabus, not in an optional module at the end.
  • They're willing to be bad at something in public for a few weeks. Everybody's first fade is rough. The ones who make it did the second one anyway.

Probably the wrong place for

Anyone after a qualification without the shop floor, or a certificate that arrives faster than the skill does. Both exist elsewhere and both cost less.

Anyone who wants to be told they're already good. That's a nice week and a wasted three months.

If that sounds like the right room, the next intake is the one to ask about.

Why here

Taught on real heads, not foam ones.

  • Real clients from week one

    Not mannequins, not week six. You're on a paying client's head with an educator stood next to you from the first week.

  • Small groups

    Small enough that someone is actually watching your hands and correcting them, not demonstrating to a room.

  • Educators still on the floor

    The people teaching you still cut in the shop every week. You're learning what works now, not what worked ten years ago.

  • Afro and textured hair, properly

    Taught as core skill, not an optional add-on module. In London, it isn't optional.

Straight answers

Questions people ask before they message.

I've never held a clipper. Am I too far behind?

No. Almost nobody arrives knowing how to use one properly, and every course is built to start from zero.

Am I too old to start?

There's no cut-off age. People train here straight out of school and twenty years into a career they've had enough of.

Do I really cut real clients, and how many?

Real clients from week one, with an educator beside you. Ask us for the number on your course and we'll give you the honest figure, not a range.

What can I actually do the day after I finish?

Cut, fade, line up and finish a full head on your own, at the pace a working shop needs. That last part is what gets you hired.

What certificate do I get?

A D&D certificate or diploma, depending on the course. If you need an internationally recognised qualification, that's what the International Certification programme is for.

What happens if I fall behind?

Tell us early and we'll work it out with you. Nobody gets pushed through a stage they haven't got yet, and nobody gets quietly written off.

Can I train around a job or family?

Send us your hours and we'll tell you straight whether the next intake fits. If it doesn't, we'll point you at the one that does.

What do I need to pay for on top of the course?

Your own tools, travel, and the time itself. Ask us before you enrol and we'll give you a realistic figure rather than let it surprise you.

What if I can't pay it all upfront?

Nobody does. A 50% deposit starts you, the rest is spread in equal instalments every two weeks while you train, 0% interest, no credit check.

Will I actually get work afterwards?

The last weeks are built around speed, because that's what shops hire for. Ask us where recent students ended up and we'll name specifics, not averages.
